The most expensive-looking woman at church last Sunday spent less on her whole outfit than most of us spend on one blouse, and nobody in that pew could have told you her jacket came off a thrift rack for eleven dollars. She wasn't wearing a single visible logo, nothing sparkled with a designer clasp, and still she looked like old money walking in for the second reading. What she understood, and what most of us were never actually taught, is that expensive-looking was never about a label sewn into a collar. It's about the weight of the fabric in your hand, the way a seam lies flat instead of puckering, the particular quiet sheen only real silk holds under church lights.

Here's the secret the resale racks are hiding in plain sight: mass-market fashion almost never gets made the way it used to, not at any price point most of us shop at regularly. But the silk blouses, the leather handbags, the wool blazers made twenty or thirty years ago were built to survive decades of wear, which means they're still out there, still structurally sound, waiting on a five-dollar rack for someone who knows what to look for.
