The third find is a leather handbag with real metal hardware, heavy clasps and buckles rather than the lightweight plastic-coated metal most new bags are finished with today. Genuine leather actually improves with age, developing a soft patina and a slight give that synthetic leather can only fake for the first few months before it starts to crack and peel at the corners. A bag with real weight to its hardware reads as established and put-together the instant you set it on a table, in a way no trendy shape or color can substitute for.

Look for a bag that feels heavier than expected for its size, check that the zipper pulls and clasps are solid metal rather than hollow, and run a finger along the seams to make sure the leather isn't peeling or flaking at the edges. A structured tote works beautifully for the office or running weekday errands. A softer leather crossbody suits an afternoon out, hands free and unfussy. And for evening, a small leather clutch with real hardware instantly elevates whatever you're wearing. If the bag feels a little tired, wrap a small silk scarf loosely around the handle, an easy refresh that costs nothing and makes a secondhand bag feel new again.
