The first find worth digging for is a structured blazer with real shoulder pads still sewn into the lining, the kind of construction almost no mass-market brand bothers with anymore because foam and padding cost money and time. Decades ago, blazers were cut with an actual architecture to them, a shoulder line built to hold its shape and a lapel that rolled instead of lying flat, and that structure does something for a changing posture that a flimsy new blazer simply can't, squaring the shoulders and giving the whole silhouette somewhere to hang from instead of drooping in on itself by noon.

When you're flipping through a rack, run your hand along the shoulder seam and feel for that firm, slightly padded resistance, then check that the lapel has a little body to it rather than folding like paper. For work, a navy or soft grey blazer thrown over trousers reads instantly credible. For running errands or meeting a friend for lunch, the same blazer in camel looks effortless over a plain pair of jeans. And for evening, slip that structured jacket over a simple black slip dress and watch it become the most tailored thing in the room. Pin a small vintage lapel pin near the collar, and suddenly the blazer looks like it was chosen for you, not just grabbed off a rack.
