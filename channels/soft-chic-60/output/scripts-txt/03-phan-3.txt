The third habit has nothing to do with fit and everything to do with color, and it's worth pausing here, because this is the one that surprises people the most. As we get older, our skin naturally loses some of its warmth and contrast, which means the colors that once looked effortless can start to sit a little flatly against the face. A pale beige that used to feel neutral and safe can, on more mature skin, drain color rather than complement it, leaving you looking tired even on days you feel wonderful.

The solution isn't to abandon neutrals, it's to choose warmer, richer versions of them: a deep ivory instead of pale beige, a forest green instead of a washed-out sage. These deeper tones reflect light back toward the face instead of absorbing it, and the difference in photographs alone can be startling. If you've ever wondered why the exact same style of sweater looks radiant on one woman and merely fine on another, color is very often the quiet answer nobody talks about. And if this is already making you rethink your own sweater drawer, that's exactly the moment to hit that like button, because it genuinely helps more women who need to hear this find their way here.
