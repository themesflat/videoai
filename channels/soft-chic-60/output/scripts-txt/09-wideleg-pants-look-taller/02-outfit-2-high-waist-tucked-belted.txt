The second formula is simple but easy to skip on a busy morning: a high-rise wide-leg pant with a fitted top tucked all the way in, cinched with a thin belt sitting right at the natural waist. Wide-leg pants have enormous visual real estate, and left untucked with a loose top drooping over the waistband, that fabric reads as one long, low-slung shape instead of two clearly defined proportions. Tucking in and belting does something almost immediate: it tells the eye exactly where your waist actually sits, high, and everything below that point gets counted as leg.

The reason this reads as taller has to do with leg-to-torso ratio rather than actual height. When the visible waist rises, the legs appear to lengthen, even though nothing about your body has physically changed. For the office, tuck a fitted button-down into the pants and add a slim leather belt in a matching neutral, understated and sharp. Out running errands, a fitted ribbed tank tucked in with a woven belt feels relaxed but still defined. For evening, a silky fitted camisole tucked in with a slim metallic belt catches light exactly where the eye should land first, your waist.

Picture catching a glimpse of yourself in a shop window mid-stride, belt cinched, top tucked, and realizing your legs look like they start almost at your ribcage, all from thirty seconds spent tucking in a top you already owned.
