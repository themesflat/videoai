The third formula is about where a jacket ends, not where it begins. Layer a cropped blazer or cardigan that stops exactly at the break point of the wide-leg pant, right where the fabric starts to flare, rather than a longer jacket that drops past the hip and covers that transition. A long jacket over wide-leg pants visually merges the two garments into one shapeless block, and a body reading as one solid block from shoulder to knee is a body that reads as shorter, no matter how tall the person actually is.

The fix works because it restores a proportion break at the correct point, roughly one-third torso to two-thirds leg, which is the ratio the eye instinctively associates with height and elegance. For the office, a cropped tweed or ponte blazer ending right at the hip crease over high-rise trousers looks sharp and deliberate. Meeting a friend for lunch, a cropped open cardigan in a soft knit does the same job more casually. For evening, a cropped structured jacket left open over a fitted top keeps that same clean break while still feeling dressed up, with a stack of thin gold bracelets pulling a little extra attention toward the wrist and away from the hip.

Picture trying on a jacket one size shorter than what you'd normally reach for, feeling almost hesitant about it in the mirror, then watching your whole silhouette snap into two clean, elegant proportions instead of one long, undefined shape.
