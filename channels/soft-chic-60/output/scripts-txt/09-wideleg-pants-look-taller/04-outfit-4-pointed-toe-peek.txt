The fourth formula lives entirely at the hem: let a pointed-toe flat or heel peek out just an inch or two below the pant leg, instead of letting the fabric puddle all the way down over a round-toe shoe. A round toe fully hidden under the hem creates a soft, blunt stopping point right at the floor, while a pointed toe extends the line of the leg past the fabric itself, adding visual length exactly where the body actually ends.

This matters more with wide-leg pants than with any other cut, because the extra fabric already has a tendency to pool at the ankle and shorten the leg from below. A slightly shorter hem, tailored to break just above the shoe rather than sweeping the floor, combined with a pointed toe, adds back nearly all of that lost length. For the office, a pointed nude flat in a shade close to your own skin tone creates the longest possible unbroken line. Out for the day, a pointed loafer does the same job with a little more structure. For evening, a pointed heel in a metallic tone catches light right at the point where the leg appears to end, drawing the eye further down and out.

Picture having your favorite wide-leg trousers hemmed just half an inch shorter, slipping on a pointed flat instead of your usual round one, and noticing for the first time that your legs seem to go on a little longer than they did yesterday, in the exact same pants.
