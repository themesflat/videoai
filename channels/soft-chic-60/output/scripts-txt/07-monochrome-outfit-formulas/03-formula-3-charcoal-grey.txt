The third formula is charcoal grey, but layered, the same exact shade repeated in two or three different fabrics rather than one flat piece of clothing. A charcoal sweater over a charcoal shirt over charcoal trousers doesn't look monotonous the way it sounds, because the different textures, a bit of wool here, a bit of knit there, catch light differently and create depth on their own, and that depth happens to sit beautifully against salt-and-pepper hair without competing for attention.

For work, three layers of charcoal, a fine knit, a blazer, and tailored trousers, reads as quietly authoritative. For a day out, drop to two layers and add crisp white shoes to keep it from feeling heavy. For evening, let silver metallics do the work, a cuff or drop earrings against all that grey. The accessory that pulls the whole thing together is a scarf in a shade just slightly lighter than the rest, enough to catch the eye without breaking the color story.

Picture stepping out in three shades of the same grey, the scarf glinting a touch lighter at your neck, looking like you thought about texture on purpose, because you did.
