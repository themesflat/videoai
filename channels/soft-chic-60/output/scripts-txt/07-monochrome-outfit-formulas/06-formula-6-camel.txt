The sixth formula is camel, worn tonally, meaning the same warm camel shade repeated across suede, wool, and smooth leather so the textures do the visual work instead of a print or a second color. Camel is one of the rare neutrals that never tips into looking too young or too aging, it simply reads as expensive, which is exactly why it's stayed a staple in tailoring for a century.

For work, a camel wool blazer over a plain white shirt with camel trousers looks quietly luxurious. For a day out, camel with denim keeps things relaxed while the texture contrast still does its job. For evening, camel from top to bottom with gold metallics feels rich without trying. The accessory to reach for is a small printed silk scarf that pulls in a few shades of camel and cream together, tied loosely, it adds just enough pattern without breaking the monochrome line.

Picture running a Saturday errand in camel suede boots, camel wool trousers, and a camel coat, the different textures catching the afternoon light, and a stranger asking where your outfit is from, assuming it must be new.
