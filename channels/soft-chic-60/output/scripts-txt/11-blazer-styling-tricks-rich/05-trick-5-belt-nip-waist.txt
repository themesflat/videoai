The fifth trick is tying a thin belt over the blazer at the natural waist instead of letting the jacket hang straight and boxy from the shoulders. Most blazers, especially the ones bought a size up for comfort or bought years ago before your shape shifted slightly, are cut to hang in a rectangle, and a rectangle on a body reads as borrowed, like something grabbed for a meeting rather than chosen with intention. A thin belt cinched at the waist reintroduces a shape the fabric alone no longer suggests.

For the office, a slim leather belt in the same tone as your shoes, worn over the blazer, turns a shapeless jacket into something that looks tailored specifically to you. Out running errands, the same belt over a more casual blazer and jeans adds structure without looking fussy. In the evening, a thin belt with a small polished buckle over an open blazer draws the eye straight to the waist under any lighting. This one small addition does more for silhouette than anything else on this list, and it works on a blazer that's slightly too big just as well as one that fits perfectly.

Picture pulling that same oversized blazer out of the closet you almost donated last spring, cinching a thin belt at the waist for the first time, and realizing it was never the blazer that needed replacing, it was simply missing one belt's worth of shape.
