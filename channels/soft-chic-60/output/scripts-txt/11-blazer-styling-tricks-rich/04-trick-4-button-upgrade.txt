The fourth trick is replacing the plastic buttons your blazer came with with proper horn or matte metal buttons from a fabric or tailoring shop, a swap that usually costs less than a coffee and takes a tailor about ten minutes. Plastic buttons catch light in a flat, slightly hollow way that the eye registers as inexpensive almost instantly, even when the rest of the jacket is genuinely well made, while horn and brushed metal have a depth and texture that photographs and reads as considered rather than mass-produced.

This tiny swap changes how the whole jacket is read in every setting you'd wear it. At the office, matte horn buttons on a navy or grey blazer look quietly deliberate under fluorescent light, in a way plastic never manages. Running errands, nobody consciously clocks a button, but they register the overall impression of quality without knowing why. For evening, buttons with a soft brushed-metal finish catch just enough warm light to look intentional rather than accidental. It's the cheapest fix on this entire list, and arguably the one with the biggest gap between effort and effect.

Picture handing your blazer to a tailor for what feels like a small, almost silly request, and picking it up a week later to find the exact same jacket somehow looking like it belongs in a completely different price bracket.
