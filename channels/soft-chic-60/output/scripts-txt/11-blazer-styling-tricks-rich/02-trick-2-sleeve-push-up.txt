The second trick is pushing or folding the sleeves up to just below the elbow instead of wearing them all the way down to the wrist. A sleeve that runs long and untouched reads as borrowed, like something pulled straight off a hanger with no thought given to it at all, while a sleeve that's been deliberately pushed up and left to sit in soft, slightly rumpled folds reads as a choice, and choices are exactly what expensive dressing is made of.

The reason it works is simple: pushing the sleeve up reveals your wrist, and a bare wrist with a watch or a single thin bangle catching the light does more visual work in that moment than the sleeve fabric ever could. At the office, push the sleeves up just enough to show a slim watch face while the blazer stays otherwise buttoned and composed. Out running errands, fold them looser and higher, closer to mid-forearm, for something more relaxed. In the evening, push them up and add one delicate bracelet that catches lamp light every time you gesture while you talk, which happens far more than you'd think.

Picture reaching for your coffee cup mid-conversation, sleeve pushed up, wrist bare except for one thin gold bangle, and noticing the person across the table glance at it for just a second longer than they meant to.
