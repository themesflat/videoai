The third trick is wearing the blazer open over an outfit that's entirely one tone underneath, a cream top with cream trousers, or soft grey with soft grey, rather than mixing several competing colors and patterns beneath it. This is the quiet luxury principle designers have used for years: the fewer colors fighting for attention in one outfit, the more expensive the whole thing looks, because the eye reads simplicity as confidence rather than as having "less to work with."

Leave the blazer open so the monochrome base shows all the way down, and let the blazer itself be the one contrasting element, in camel over cream, or charcoal over soft grey. For the office, this reads as quietly powerful rather than trying hard. Running errands, the same monochrome base under an open blazer looks put-together even in the most casual fabric, like jeans in a matching wash. For evening, a monochrome slip in bronze or deep green under an open blazer, with one statement earring, looks curated rather than accidental. The trick isn't buying more clothes, it's simply not layering three different colors under the one piece meant to stand out.

Picture standing at the mirror on a morning you almost reached for a patterned blouse out of habit, choosing the plain cream one instead, and watching the whole outfit suddenly look like it came from somewhere far more expensive than your own closet.
