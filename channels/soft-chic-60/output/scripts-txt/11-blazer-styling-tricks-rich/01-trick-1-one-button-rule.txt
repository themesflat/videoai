The first trick is the one-button rule: when you close your blazer, fasten only the middle button, or the top button if yours is a single-button style, and never, ever close every button down the front. Buttoning a blazer all the way up is the single fastest way to make good tailoring look cheap, because it flattens the lapel roll, squares off the waist into a box, and removes the small gap of negative space that makes a jacket look like it was cut for your body instead of pulled off a rack.

This one matters everywhere you'd wear a blazer at all. At the office, closing just the middle button over a simple blouse instantly creates a nipped-in silhouette that reads as tailored rather than borrowed. Running errands, leave it open entirely over a plain tee and let it move with you instead of boxing you in. For evening, that same single closed button over a slip dress creates a waistline the eye can actually find in a room full of shapeless coats. The fix costs nothing, it's simply a matter of which button your fingers reach for.

Picture catching your reflection in a shop window on your way to a meeting, one button closed, the lapel rolling open just slightly at the chest, and realizing the woman looking back doesn't look like she's wearing a jacket for the occasion, she looks like the jacket was made to hang exactly that way on her.
