The fourth habit hides in the sleeves, and it's born from a very understandable impulse: covering the hands, which many women feel show age more honestly than almost anywhere else. But sleeves that fall past the wrist and swallow the hand do something unintended. They make the whole arm look passive, even sluggish, because hidden hands read, visually, as hands with nothing to do.

Pushing the sleeve up to three-quarter length, or folding back a cuff just once, changes that instantly. The wrist becomes visible again, a slim watch or a single ring catches the light, and the whole gesture of the arm starts to look active and engaged rather than tucked away. It's strange how much confidence can live in a wrist, but watch any woman who carries herself with quiet self-assurance, and you'll notice her sleeves are rarely covering her hands completely. This is one of those fixes you can do in the three seconds it takes to fold back a cuff before you leave the house, and the difference in how put-together you look is completely disproportionate to the effort involved.
