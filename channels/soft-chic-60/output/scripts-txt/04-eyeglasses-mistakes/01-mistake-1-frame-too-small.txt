The first mistake is holding onto a frame that fit perfectly ten or fifteen years ago, back when your face had sharper, more defined lines through the jaw and cheekbones. As the years pass, the face naturally softens, a little fullness settles into the cheeks, the jawline rounds slightly, and features that were once crisp become gentler overall. A small frame that used to sit neatly within your face now looks lost on it, swallowed by all that softened space, and instead of framing your eyes it just seems to shrink into the middle of your face, making the whole look feel underwhelming rather than sharp.

The fix is simpler than it sounds: go up one size from what you're used to, and let the top of the frame sit close to your brows rather than below them, so the frame actually claims the real estate your face is offering it now. For the office, a slightly squarer frame in a neutral tone adds quiet structure without shouting for attention. For errands and everyday outings, a bigger round frame softens things further and feels effortless with a simple sweater or blazer. For evening, a frame with a subtle metallic accent along the temple catches just enough light to feel a touch more polished, especially paired with a thin glasses chain that rests along the collarbone like a piece of jewelry rather than something purely functional.

Picture standing at the bathroom mirror trying on a new pair one size up for the first time, expecting to feel overwhelmed by the size, and instead watching your whole face seem to open up, your eyes suddenly the center of attention again instead of an afterthought behind a frame that had quietly stopped fitting the woman looking back at you.
