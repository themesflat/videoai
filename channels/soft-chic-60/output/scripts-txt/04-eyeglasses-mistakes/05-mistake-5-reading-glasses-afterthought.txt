The fifth mistake is treating reading glasses like a chore rather than an accessory, grabbing the cheapest pair off a drugstore rack and leaving three or four identical ones scattered around the house, one by the bed, one in the kitchen, one buried at the bottom of a bag, all slightly bent and none of them chosen with any real thought behind it. There's a quiet psychology to this: reading glasses often get treated as a symbol of losing something rather than gaining a useful tool, and that feeling shows, both in how careless the glasses themselves look and in the slightly resigned way many women reach for them in public, almost apologizing for needing them at all.

The fix is deciding that reading glasses deserve the same consideration as any other accessory you own, investing in one or two frames you genuinely find attractive rather than a drawer full of forgettable ones, and pairing them with a glasses chain that functions as jewelry rather than a purely practical afterthought. At home, a slim delicate chain keeps a favorite pair within reach without them ever going missing again. For work, a foldable pair tucked into a smart little case in your bag means you're never caught rifling through a tote in front of colleagues. For evening reading, whether it's a menu or a place card, a frame with a subtle patterned accent on the temple turns a purely functional moment into one that still looks intentional and put together.

Picture reaching for reading glasses at a restaurant table the old way, an old bent drugstore pair, versus reaching for a beautiful frame on a chain around your neck instead, lifting it to your eyes with the easy confidence of someone wearing a necklace, not someone quietly hiding a task she'd rather skip altogether.
