The third mistake is wearing the same lenses year after year, long past the point they were still doing your eyes any favors, without realizing that lenses quietly yellow, scratch, and dull with time the same way an old windshield does. You'd never notice it happening day to day, it's far too gradual for that, but the cumulative effect is lenses that soften and blur the very thing people are trying to look at when they talk to you, your eyes, leaving them looking tired or hazy even on mornings when you feel perfectly rested and well slept.

The fix is treating your lenses the way you'd treat anything else with a shelf life: replace them, or at minimum add a light anti-glare coating, roughly every two years, rather than waiting until the prescription itself starts to feel wrong. This isn't really about the occasion, because dull, yellowed lenses work against you everywhere, at the office under fluorescent light, out running errands in bright sun, and especially in the evening under warm restaurant lighting, where a fresh anti-glare coating keeps your eyes looking clear and alert instead of glazed over in every photo taken that night. Keeping a dedicated lens cloth in your bag rather than reaching for a shirt hem or a paper napkin matters more than it seems, since scratches build up exactly the way discoloration does, quietly and permanently, one small wipe at a time.

Picture leaning in close to the mirror one afternoon, cleaning your lenses out of habit, and noticing for the first time a faint yellow cast you'd simply stopped seeing because it crept in so slowly, then picture how different your whole face looks minutes later in a fresh pair, your eyes suddenly clear and present again instead of sitting quietly behind a haze you'd mistaken for just getting older.
