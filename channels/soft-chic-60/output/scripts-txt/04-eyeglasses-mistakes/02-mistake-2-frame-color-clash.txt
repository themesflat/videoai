The second mistake is reaching for the same brown or black frame you've worn for years, the one that matched your hair beautifully back when it was still its original color. Hair doesn't change overnight, it shifts slowly, month by month, until one day it's mostly silver or a soft salt-and-pepper blend, and somewhere in that slow transition the warm brown or deep black frame that once blended in so seamlessly starts to sit against your face like a hard, dark line instead, fighting the very color it used to complement so well.

The fix is to let your frame color evolve the same way your hair did, shifting toward silver, smoky grey, or a pale tortoise that sits gently against lighter hair instead of competing with it. For work, a smoky grey frame reads polished and a little more serious without feeling harsh under office light. For being out and about, a pale tortoise softens everything and pairs beautifully with almost anything already in your closet. For evening, a frame in a silver metallic finish catches the light exactly the way fine jewelry does, and pairing it with matching silver-tone earrings or a scarf with a touch of silver thread pulls the whole look together instead of leaving the frame to stand alone against your face.

Imagine catching your reflection in the mirror after finally switching from your old brown frame to a smoky grey one, half expecting it to feel like a small, forgettable change, and instead noticing your whole face looks brighter, more current, more like the woman you are right now rather than a slightly outdated photograph of who you used to be.
