The fourth mistake is a sleeve that lands right at the widest part of the forearm, exactly where the fabric hem creates a small horizontal line that visually shortens and widens the arm without your ever realizing why the sleeve never quite feels flattering in photos. It's rarely the arm itself that's the problem, it's the hem sitting in the one spot on the entire arm that draws a stopping line exactly where you don't want one.

Choosing a three-quarter sleeve pushed slightly higher on the forearm, a full long sleeve instead, or a sleeve that ends right at the narrowest point just above the wrist bone, moves that hemline off the widest point and elongates the whole arm in the process. A loose bracelet or a simple watch worn right at that new hemline draws the eye to the narrowest part of the wrist and adds one more small, deliberate detail.

Picture the same blouse with the sleeve simply pushed an inch higher, or rolled once and left loose rather than tight, and notice how much longer and leaner your arm suddenly looks in exactly the same fabric, on exactly the same body, with nothing else about the blouse having changed at all.
