The sixth and final mistake is reaching for a blouse in a fabric that looks beautiful ironed at eight in the morning and creased and tired by noon, usually a pure cotton or pure linen with nothing else blended in to help it hold its shape. The wrinkling itself isn't really the issue, it's what it quietly does to the impression you left that morning, undoing all the effort you put in before anyone even sees you again later in the afternoon or at dinner.

A cotton-poly blend, a silk-blend, or a good quality rayon blouse holds its shape and stays crisp through a full day of errands, lunch, and everything after without any extra effort on your part, so the put-together woman who left the house at nine is still exactly who people see at three. When you're shopping, checking the fabric content on the tag for even a small percentage of synthetic fiber blended in, five or ten percent is often enough, tells you in seconds whether a blouse will survive a full day.

Imagine wearing the exact same style of blouse, just in a blend instead of pure cotton, and catching your reflection at the end of a long day looking every bit as fresh and finished as you did standing in front of the mirror at breakfast, with nothing in between having gone the way you expected.
