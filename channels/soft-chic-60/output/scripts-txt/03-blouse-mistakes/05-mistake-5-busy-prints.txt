The fifth habit is choosing a bold, busy print for a blouse without considering that it sits directly beneath your face, the one spot on your whole body where the eye naturally goes first before anywhere else. A loud, competing pattern pulls attention to texture and noise instead of to your eyes and your expression, and it can visually overwhelm rather than flatter, no matter how much you genuinely love the pattern itself sitting on the hanger in the store.

Saving bold prints for a scarf, a bag, or an accessory a little further from the face, and choosing a solid color or a small, quiet, tone-on-tone print for the blouse itself, lets your face stay the focal point it should be. A floral you love in bright coral, for example, works far better draped as a scarf over a plain ivory blouse than it does as the blouse itself, and the same goes for bold stripes, better as a bag or a pair of shoes than as the fabric closest to your chin.

Imagine the same busy pattern you love, worn instead as a scarf draped loosely over a plain blouse, and noticing how much calmer, more intentional, and more put-together the whole look feels, while the print still gets to be part of your day.
