The second mistake is buttoning a blouse all the way to the very top button, the way we were taught looked "proper," without noticing what it quietly does to the face above it. A collar closed tight at the throat boxes in the neckline and reads prim and severe rather than soft, and because the eye reads a closed, high collar as formal and guarded, it can make even a relaxed, friendly expression look a little more tense and a little older than it actually is. It's a strange trade: the button meant to look "finished" ends up finishing the wrong impression.

Undoing just the top one or two buttons, choosing a blouse with a soft V or a gentle scoop neckline in the first place, or adding a delicate pendant necklace that draws the eye slightly downward and outward, opens up the whole face instantly. A thin gold chain works beautifully under an open collar for daytime, while a slightly longer pendant softens the look even further for an evening version of the same blouse.

Picture the same blouse, the same morning, with just one button left open and a small necklace resting just below the collarbone, and notice how much softer, warmer, and more approachable your whole expression suddenly looks back at you in the mirror, without a single other thing having changed.
