The first habit is reaching for the blouse with the most room to spare, because a little extra fabric feels forgiving and safe. But a boxy, oversized blouse with nothing to mark your waist settles into one flat rectangle from shoulder to hip, and instead of skimming your shape, it quietly erases it, leaving the eye nowhere to land except on the fabric itself rather than on you.

The fix takes seconds: a half-tuck at the front, or a thin belt worn loosely over the blouse at your natural waist, gives the eye a place to rest and turns the exact same blouse into something that looks chosen rather than grabbed off the hanger. Imagine standing in front of the mirror, tucking in just the front hem, and watching a blouse that felt like a paper bag suddenly show the shape of the woman wearing it.
