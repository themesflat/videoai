The first habit is reaching for the blouse with the most room to spare, because a little extra fabric feels forgiving and safe, like it's doing you a favor by hiding what you don't want to think about. But a boxy, oversized blouse with nothing to mark your waist settles into one flat rectangle from shoulder to hip, and instead of skimming your shape, it quietly erases it, leaving the eye nowhere to land except on the fabric itself rather than on you. The irony is that the very thing meant to feel safe ends up adding the visual weight it was supposed to hide.

The fix takes seconds and costs nothing extra: a half-tuck at the front, a thin belt worn loosely over the blouse at your natural waist, or simply gathering a small handful of fabric at one hip and letting it blouse slightly, all give the eye a place to rest and turn the exact same blouse into something that looks chosen rather than grabbed off the hanger. A soft fabric belt in a neutral tone works with almost any blouse color, while a slim leather belt adds a slightly more polished, tailored edge for days you want to look a touch more put together.

Imagine standing in front of the mirror on an ordinary Tuesday, tucking in just the front hem, and watching a blouse that felt like a paper bag suddenly show the shape of the woman wearing it, the same fabric, the same morning, just three seconds different.
