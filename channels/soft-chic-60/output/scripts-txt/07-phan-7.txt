The seventh and final habit is the quietest of them all, and it has nothing to do with style choices and everything to do with the condition of the sweater itself. A stretched-out neckline, fabric covered in small pills, sleeves that have lost their shape after years of wear, these details are small individually, but together they send a message before you've said a single word: that this particular piece has been neglected. And the strange truth is that a beautifully fitted, high quality sweater in a flattering color can be undone entirely by simply looking tired.

This isn't a case for buying more. It's a case for owning fewer sweaters of genuinely good fabric, a real wool and cotton blend rather than something thin and synthetic, and retiring the ones that have quietly stopped doing their job. A sweater that still holds its shape says something about how much care you put into the things around you, and that impression forms in seconds, long before anyone consciously registers why.
