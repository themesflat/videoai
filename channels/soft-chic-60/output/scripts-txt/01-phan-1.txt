The first habit is one almost every woman falls into without noticing, and it starts with a completely reasonable instinct: choosing the sweater with room to spare, because room feels safe. But heavy knit fabric doesn't drape the way a blouse does. When it hangs loose with nothing to define your waist, it settles into one wide rectangle from shoulder to hip, and the eye has nowhere to rest except on that shape. What used to read as comfortable at forty can quietly read as shapeless at sixty, not because anything about your body is wrong, but because softer skin needs a little more structure to hold its own shape, not less.

The fix is almost embarrassingly simple. Tuck the front just slightly into a pair of tailored trousers, or wrap a thin leather belt over the sweater at the narrowest point of your waist, even if you leave it loose rather than cinched. That one small line does something remarkable: it gives the eye a place to land, and suddenly the exact same sweater reads as an outfit with intention behind it, rather than something you reached for without thinking. Imagine standing in front of the mirror, adding nothing but a slim belt, and watching a sweater that felt like loungewear turn into something you'd wear to lunch with friends and feel entirely put together in.
