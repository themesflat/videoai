The sixth mistake is a color decision most women never think about twice: dark boots worn under light, cream, or ivory pants, creating a hard, high-contrast line right where the pant meets the boot. That sharp break does something visually unkind — it chops the leg into two obvious segments and makes the whole leg look shorter than it is, no matter how good the fit of either piece happens to be.

Matching your boot tone closer to your pant hem, or to your own skin tone, removes that harsh line and lets the eye travel the full length of the leg without interruption. Picture the same cream trousers, swapped from a black boot to a warm caramel or tan boot instead, and watching your legs suddenly look inches longer in a photo taken the exact same way.
