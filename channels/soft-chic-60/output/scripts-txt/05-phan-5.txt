The fifth habit shows up when the whole outfit blends into one soft, safe tone, a cream sweater over cream trousers, for example, chosen because nothing about it could possibly clash. But when nothing contrasts, nothing anchors the eye either, and the overall effect can look less like a considered outfit and more like something worn around the house. This is especially true as hair lightens with age, because a monochrome pale outfit next to pale hair can wash the whole picture out.

The fix costs nothing beyond opening the closet to a different pairing: that same cream sweater over navy or charcoal trousers instead, with one accessory, a deep red handbag, a patterned scarf, pulling everything together. Suddenly there's a place for the eye to travel, a beginning and an end to the outfit instead of one long stretch of sameness. It's remarkable how a single color swap in the bottom half of an outfit can be the fastest transformation on this entire list, turning something that felt like loungewear into something that feels genuinely dressed.
